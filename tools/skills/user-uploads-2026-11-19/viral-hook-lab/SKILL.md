# Viral Hook Lab

## Goal
Write a first-second hook that earns attention honestly, followed by a reason to keep watching. Optimize clarity and originality instead of clickbait. These are creative heuristics, NOT guaranteed reach formulas.

## Audience and channels
- English-speaking international viewers.
- Channel A: faceless football player guessing games, surprising career trajectories, dramatic reveals.
- Channel B: faceless animated science, human biology, and grounded 'what-if' thought experiments.
- Output should be compatible with vertical 9:16 short video.

## Procedure
1. Ask for or infer channel, topic, actual facts, audience, and target runtime. If missing, proceed using 15–25 seconds for football, 20–35 seconds for science.
2. Determine ONE central curiosity gap based on verified facts; do not introduce unsourced dramatic biography or implausible scientific claims.
3. Write 12 distinct opening hooks in four groups: puzzle, surprising contrast, stakes, and visual-first.
4. Make each hook speakable in roughly 1–2 seconds where feasible (typically 5–12 words). A visual cue may carry half the information.
5. Rank the best 3 by immediate comprehension, authentic curiosity, originality, and suitability for a visual opening. State why the top one is strongest.
6. For the top 3, provide: spoken words; screen text (<=8 words); first-frame visual; next line that delivers the promise; possible misleading-risk correction.
7. Suggest a controlled A/B test varying ONLY the opening (same video length, payoff, and format). Do not claim posting near a specific hour guarantees reach.

## Quality gate
- No 'Wait until the end', 'You won't believe', long brand introductions, or dishonest withholding of essential facts.
- No copyright-infringing instructions to repost third-party match highlights.
- Avoid the same generic 'Did you know...' opening across episodes.
- If a number or fact isn't verified, flag it for confirmation.

## Output
### 12 hooks
### Top 3 and why
### Ready-to-record first 3 seconds
### One clean A/B experiment
